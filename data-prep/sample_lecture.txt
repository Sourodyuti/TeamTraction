[00:00] Welcome everyone. Today we're covering backpropagation — how neural networks actually learn.
Let me start with the big picture, then we'll work through the math.

[00:15] A neural network is just a stack of weighted sums followed by nonlinear activations.
During the forward pass, input flows through these layers and produces a prediction.

[00:30] We compare that prediction to the true answer using a loss function —
usually mean squared error or cross-entropy. The loss tells us how wrong we are.

[00:45] Now here's where it gets interesting. To reduce the loss, we need to know
how each weight in the network contributed to the error. That's what backprop computes.

[01:00] Backpropagation uses the chain rule from calculus. The chain rule lets us
break apart a complicated function into simpler pieces and multiply their derivatives.

[01:15] Here's the tricky part. The chain rule says: if y depends on z, and z depends on x,
then the derivative of y with respect to x is the product of the individual derivatives.

[01:30] In a deep network, we apply this layer by layer, from the output back to the input.
Each layer's gradient depends on the gradient of the layer above it.

[01:45] This is the moment most students get lost. The chain rule across many layers
means the gradients multiply together, and this is where vanishing and exploding gradients come from.

[02:00] Let's make it concrete. Suppose our loss is the square of the prediction error.
The derivative of the loss with respect to the prediction is just twice the error.

[02:15] Then we chain that with the derivative of the activation function, and then
with the derivative of the weighted sum. Multiply them all together, layer by layer.

[02:30] That's the whole algorithm. Forward pass to compute the prediction and loss,
then backward pass to compute gradients using the chain rule, then update the weights.

[02:45] Questions? The most common confusion here is the order of multiplication
in the chain rule and why the gradients can vanish. Let's pause and make sure that's clear.
